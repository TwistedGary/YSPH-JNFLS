Infectious Diseases

Understand common infectious diseases through transmission and prevention.

01 / EXPLAINED

What are infectious diseases?

Infectious diseases are caused by pathogens such as bacteria, viruses, fungi or parasites. Their illnesses, symptoms and risks differ, so one symptom alone cannot tell you what infection a person has.

02 / EXPLAINED

Transmission differs by disease

Pathogens can spread through people, animals, food or water, vectors and the environment; one disease may have more than one route. Knowing a specific disease's route helps people choose suitable prevention.

03 / EXPLAINED

Prevention fits the route

Vaccination, ventilation, hand hygiene, food and water safety, vector control and timely care can all matter in public health. The right measures depend on the disease, setting and local health guidance.

COMMON MISCONCEPTION

Do all infectious diseases spread quickly between people?

No. Whether a disease spreads, how it spreads and its risk all depend on the specific pathogen and setting. Avoid labelling people or judging risk from online messages alone.

One thing to put into practice

Follow reliable notices from your school or local health authority, and seek professional care for persistent or severe symptoms.
