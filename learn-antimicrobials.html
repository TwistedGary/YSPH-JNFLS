Antimicrobial Resistance

Why are some infections becoming harder to treat?

Sources checked: 8 September 2026 · YSPH educational overview

On this page

What resistance meansIt is the microbeWhy public health mattersCommon misconceptionReferences
01 / EXPLAINED

What resistance means

Resistance occurs when bacteria, viruses, fungi or parasites no longer respond to antimicrobial medicines. Infections can become harder to treat.

02 / EXPLAINED

It is the microbe

Resistance concerns microbes, not a body becoming used to medicine. Resistant pathogens can spread, so the effects extend beyond an individual medicine user.

03 / EXPLAINED

Why public health matters

Misuse and overuse drive resistance. Clean water, sanitation, infection prevention and access to appropriate diagnostics and medicines also matter.

COMMON MISCONCEPTION

Does more medicine mean more protection?

No. Unnecessary or inappropriate medicines can contribute to resistance.

One thing to put into practice

Confirm the purpose and instructions with a clinician or pharmacist; do not alter treatment yourself.
